# Phase 3 — Simulation Hardliner Critique (world logic & AI believability)

Method: read src/world.js, src/battle.js, src/data.js in full; cross-checked every claim in coherence-audit.md and mvp-verify.md, then went past them via live `window.__g`/`window.game.step()` probes in a fresh tab (force-reloaded, cache-busted per harness instructions). Every finding below has a live-probe result, a code citation, or both. Two screenshots saved: `shots/sim_raid_deploy_backwards.jpg`, `shots/sim_parties_overlap_no_collision.jpg`.

**Score: 6/10** (bar: 9/10 — not met, and not close)

The systems that were *added* this pass (terrain collision, frozen garrisons, directional battles, defeat-to-nearest-settlement, per-village specialties) are honestly well-built and I could not break any of them beyond the already-known river-freeze edge case. But the pass optimized for "does the new system work in isolation" and under-asked "does this system's behavior make sense given who's alive in the world and what they just did." The result is a world where the single most common verb in the game — riding up and pressing E to raid a camp — triggers a sequence (garrison politely forms ranks for 8 seconds) that is backwards from what happened (you ambushed them), where the "locked" final boss is already sending war parties across the map before it's unlocked, and where a signature new feature (nearest-settlement defeat recovery) doesn't obey the terrain rules this same pass just introduced. Those are not edge cases — they are the default case, encountered on the first camp raid of any playthrough.

## Ranked findings

### 1. Every player-initiated attack gives the "surprised" side a full, undisturbed 8s formup — initiative is assigned backwards [Breaks-immersion]
The deploy window (`battle.js:123`, comment: *"the enemy forms up before advancing...Ambushes grant none — that is what makes them ambushes"*) is gated on a single `ambush` boolean that is `true` in exactly one case: a roaming party whose `mood==='chase'` catches you (`world.js:481`, `ambushed = p.mood === 'chase'`). It is `false` for every case where **you** are the one springing the attack:
- **Raiding a bandit camp** — `world.js:394-420` calls `startBattle(comp, ..., 'camp', false, ...)`, hardcoded `ambush=false`. Live-verified: teleported hero next to `c1`, pressed E to raid, resulting battle had `deployT: 8`, `ambush: false` — the garrison gets a full 8 seconds to "form their line" against a rider who just charged their camp. Screenshot: `shots/sim_raid_deploy_backwards.jpg`.
- **Running down a fleeing party** — a weak party in `mood:'flee'` that you catch (`world.js:477`, `canClash && dh<46`) triggers `ambushed = p.mood==='chase'` → `false`, so a party you *chased to the ground* still gets the polite formup. Live-verified: strong hero (10 knights) vs. a lone wolf in `flee` mood, caught after ~4s pursuit — resulting battle `deployT: 8, ambush: false`.
- Only the reverse case (a **stronger enemy party** running **you** down) counts as an "ambush." A raid you initiate and a chase you win are mechanically indistinguishable from a chance meeting on the road.
**Fix:** ambush should track *who* had the element of surprise, not just "was it a chasing party." Camp raids and successful hero-initiated pursuits should set `ambush:true` (or a symmetrical "you struck first" flag that skips/shortens `deployT` and gives the hero's side the pincer/rear-spawn treatment instead of the enemy).
**Effort:** S — swap the two `ambush:false` call sites to derive the flag from who closed the distance, no new systems needed.

### 2. Wolfsjaw Hold already fields active war parties from turn one — the "locked, feeding on its camps" fiction is false from the first frame [Breaks-immersion]
The gate toast reads *"Wolfsjaw won't fall while its camps still feed it — cut the supply lines first"* and the stronghold's garrison famously only rolls once camps 1-3 are razed (verified working in mvp-verify). But that's only the stronghold's *defensive* garrison. Its *offensive* roaming parties are live from the start:
- `World` constructor spawn loop (`world.js:60-66`): `const n = c.stronghold ? 2 : 1 + (rng()*2|0)` — the stronghold gets its own roaming parties in the initial spawn, same as any feeder camp, no gating on `save.won`/razed-count.
- The 40s respawn timer (`world.js:492-508`) treats `'strong'` as just another entry in `alive` and can pick it via `alive[(rng()*alive.length)|0]` for the entire game, again with no stronghold-specific gate.
- Live-verified at a fresh game start: `parties` already contained 2 parties tagged `camp:'strong'` (one comp: `bandit,raider,bandit,wolf,bandit,bandit`, strength 6) able to chase/ambush the player exactly like a feeder-camp party, hours before the 3 camps are razed.
A "sealed, waiting fortress" doesn't send patrols. Either Wolfsjaw's field parties should stay home until the siege condition unlocks (matching the "won't fall / hasn't mustered" framing), or the framing needs to admit the hold already has forces in play (weakening the "cut supply lines" motivation, since it's evidently not starving).
**Fix:** simplest coherent fix — exclude `stronghold` camps from `spawnParty`/the periodic respawn loop until `save.won` is reachable (i.e., until razedCount hits 3), so Wolfsjaw truly sits dormant behind its "still feeds it" excuse until unlocked, then musters both its frozen defense garrison and its first roaming parties together.
**Effort:** S — one guard clause in the initial spawn loop and the respawn picker.

### 3. The "bandit remnants rally" fiction has zero mechanical link to the remnants — Wolfsjaw's garrison ignores what happened at camps 1-3 entirely [Breaks-immersion] *(re-verifies and sharpens a prior audit item)*
`rollGarrison` for any camp, including `strong`, is `target = max(camp.size+2, round(mine * (camp.tier||1)))`, seeded by `camp.x*31+camp.y*7+mine*13` (`world.js:191-206`). Nothing in that formula reads the composition, size, or fate of camps 1/2/3. Live-verified: called `rollGarrison(strongCamp)` twice at the same `mine` value — byte-identical output both times, and the formula structurally cannot vary with "how many bandits fled here" because it never looks at any other camp's data.
This means a playthrough where camps 1-3 were tiny (raided at level 1, minimal garrisons) and one where they were maximally built up produces the **exact same** Wolfsjaw garrison, provided the player's own strength at the 3rd-camp moment matches. The toast *"the bandit remnants rally at Wolfsjaw"* asserts a causal story ("survivors from the camps you burned regroup here") the mechanic doesn't tell — it's the same `mine × tier` scaling every other camp already uses, just with a bigger tier constant.
**Fix:** either make the fiction true — sum a fraction of the actual `strength()` of the 3 razed garrisons into Wolfsjaw's target before applying the mine-based floor — or drop "remnants rally" for language that doesn't claim a causal link the numbers don't have ("Wolfsjaw musters for the coming siege").
**Effort:** S (copy-only) to M (thread razed-garrison totals into the roll for the mechanically-true fix).

### 4. Roaming parties have no collision with each other, camps, or settlements — a village is a solid-looking void [Breaks-immersion]
The terrain-collision pass (audited/verified as excellent for hero-vs-mountain and hero-vs-river) never extended to party-vs-anything-but-terrain:
- **Party vs. party:** placed two parties 10px apart with converging wander goals and ran 2 sim-seconds — they ended 6px apart, having passed through/into each other with zero separation force (compare: battle.js has an explicit separation loop for exactly this purpose in combat, at ~line 543 — the concept exists, it just isn't applied on the world map).
- **Party/hero vs. settlements & camps:** `blockedAt()` only checks `riverSegs`/`bridgePts`/`solids` (mountains+rocks). Live-verified `blockedAt(700,1150)` (Ashford's exact drawn coordinates, keep-with-towers art) and `blockedAt(1050,1500)` (camp `c1`'s tents) both return `false`. Drove a party in a dead-straight line from `(550,1150)` to `(850,1150)` — it passed directly through Ashford's rendered walls without a single pixel of deflection (path logged perfectly linear every 15 frames).
A drawn palisade fort, three-house village, and windmill read as solid architecture but are pure paint — weaker collision than the smallest decorative rock. Screenshot: `shots/sim_parties_overlap_no_collision.jpg` (two parties nearly stacked, mid-overlap, right at Ashford).
**Fix:** add settlements/camps to (or alongside) `this.solids` with a modest radius, and add the same pairwise separation math from battle.js to the world-map party loop (cheap: O(n²) over at most ~10 parties).
**Effort:** M.

### 5. "Carried to the nearest settlement" on defeat uses straight-line distance, ignoring the very river/bridge rules this pass introduced [Breaks-immersion]
The defeat branch (`world.js:306-311`) picks `nearest` by raw `dist2` (Euclidean) over `WORLD.settlements`, with no path-cost or reachability check against `blockedAt`/bridges. Live-verified: placed the hero at `(1100,1150)` — the east bank of the west river, confirmed live that the straight segment from `(1100,1150)` to Ashford `(700,1150)` is river-blocked at multiple sampled points (no bridge within reach at that y; bridges sit at y=640 and y=1655) — then forced a defeat. Result: `save.x/y` teleported to Ashford `(700,1230)` anyway, silently crossing an "impassable" river with no bridge, in the same update that this pass's headline feature (defeat-carries-you-somewhere-sensible) is supposed to be selling as a fix to the old "always teleports to Ashford" bug. It swapped one incoherent teleport (always Ashford) for a narrower but still-present one (nearest-as-the-crow-flies, which regularly is on the wrong side of a river).
**Fix:** either restrict the nearest-settlement search to reachable candidates (walk each settlement's straight line against `blockedAt`, or precompute which side of each river each settlement sits on and only compare distances within the same side, falling back to the nearest bridge crossing's cost otherwise), or add a cheap toast admission ("carried across the ford at night") if a real path-aware fix is out of scope this pass.
**Effort:** M — needs a same-side check or a coarse path-cost, not full pathfinding.

### 6. Razing a camp changes almost nothing about the world except a future spawn-cap decrement [Feels-prototype]
On raze: the camp's own already-tagged parties are removed (`save.parties.filter(p=>p.camp!==camp.id)`, `world.js:399`) and the *future* party cap drops from `2+alive.length*2` (`world.js:495`). That's the entire footprint. Checked and ruled out as unaffected: recruit prices at nearby villages (`costAt` reads only per-settlement static fields, never camp state), any "safer roads" signal (no such thing exists — roads have no traffic/danger stat), any toast/reaction at the nearest village, and critically — **other camps' (including Wolfsjaw's, per finding #2) already-spawned parties are completely untouched**, so even clearing all 3 feeder camps doesn't reduce the number of hostile parties already roaming from the ones still alive plus Wolfsjaw. The world doesn't notice you cleared a camp beyond a slow trickle-down in future spawn odds.
**Fix:** cheapest real signal — a one-time toast/particle at the nearest settlement ("word reaches the village — the roads are safer") plus a small, explicit reduction to existing party count near the razed camp's old territory (not just future spawns).
**Effort:** S/M.

### 7. Fleeing parties that escape have no memory — they instantly resume anonymous patrol as if nothing happened [Feels-prototype]
Once `dh` exceeds engagement range or the hero enters a safe zone, `p.mood` resets to `null` (`world.js:438`) and the party falls straight back into `p.wander` around `p.home` (`world.js:440-445`) — identical behavior to a party that was never spotted. There is no cooldown, no elevated alert state, no "beeline home to report," nothing that reflects "I nearly died a second ago." Combined with finding #4 (no memory of terrain either), a fled party's entire experience resets the instant distance opens up.
**Fix:** cheapest coherent touch — a short `p.spooked` timer after disengaging that biases `p.wander` toward `p.home` more tightly (running for the den) instead of the normal wide wander radius, so a scare visibly changes behavior for a beat.
**Effort:** S.

### 8. The known off-axis river-freeze bug (mvp-verify's "still broken") still reproduces exactly as documented, and a permanently-stuck pursuer is a harder believability failure than its rarity suggests [Breaks-immersion — re-ranked up]
mvp-verify accepted this at 8/10 as "a narrower edge case, not fully eliminated." Re-ran the exact repro (chase party and hero level with each other, offset from the bridge's transverse position by 120-160px): party correctly retargets toward the bridge, closes most of the distance, then **freezes permanently at `(985,760)`** — reproduced again live, static for the full 10 simulated seconds I ran it (previous report ran 300s with the same result). I'm re-ranking this from "acceptable narrow edge case" to a blocking finding for a 9/10 bar: the frequency of the trigger angle is irrelevant to the believability lens — *any* reachable configuration where a hunting predator visibly walks up to a river and then just stops forever, forever, is a "the AI is broken" read for any player who sees it, and rivers running most of the map's height means the odds of a random chase landing in that off-axis band over a full playthrough are not actually low.
**Fix:** per mvp-verify's own diagnosis — replace the two disjoint circles (22px block radius, 62-95px bridge exemption) with a continuous distance-based falloff so no dead annulus exists between them, at any approach angle.
**Effort:** M (already scoped in the prior report; still not shipped).

### 9. Re-ranking: roads are still 100% invisible to every NPC in the world — audit item #12 was only half-fixed [Feels-prototype — re-ranked up from "mostly fixed, cosmetic gap"]
mvp-verify closed audit item #12 as "roads are functionally real (+15% speed), Brindle-Coldwell gap is the only remaining piece, doesn't block." That verification only ever tested the **hero's** speed. Grepped the party movement loop (`world.js:424-472`) and the single `onRoad()` call site (`world.js:331`) — it is called exactly once in the entire file, inside the hero's own movement block. No roaming party ever calls `onRoad`, gets a speed bonus on one, or paths toward one. Bandits, raiders, and wolves cut cross-country in perfectly straight lines between `home` and `wander` regardless of whether a road runs right past them. For a lens that asks "does the AI behave like it lives here," a road network only one resident of the world can see or use is a bigger gap than the missing Brindle-Coldwell segment that got all the attention.
**Fix:** apply the same `onRoad(p.x,p.y)` speed check to party movement (reuses the existing helper, no new geometry).
**Effort:** S.

### 4. Bandit parties get the hero's +15% road speed — VERIFIED, exact ratio
Pinned a party's position each frame (to hold `onRoad()` constant while velocity converges) at a clean on-road point (`1500,1150`, on the Ashford–Highmere road, clear of both rivers) vs. an off-road point directly south (`1500,1700`): terminal speed **92.0 on-road vs. 80.0 off-road — ratio 1.150**, matching the hero's own bonus exactly. Confirmed live via `w.onRoad()`/`w.blockedAt()` that both probe points are correctly classified and neither sits in a river band. Closes finding #9 (roads invisible to AI) cleanly.
- **Side observation, not a regression:** while hunting for a clean on-road test point I found that the Ashford–Coldwell road segment (a straight line between the two settlement centers) passes directly through the west river's blocked band at a non-bridge point (`blockedAt(1025,850)` returns `true` while `onRoad(1025,850)` also returns `true`) — the road and the river disagree with each other at that stretch. It has no gameplay consequence today (parties re-route around any blocked point regardless of whether it's "on a road," and the hero simply couldn't stand there to get the bonus), so I'm not ranking it as a finding, just flagging it as latent inconsistency in the hand-authored road/river layout worth a look if roads ever become path-plannable.

### 5. Catch-all unstick — NOT VERIFIED. The exact repro from mvp-verify still freezes the party permanently. [Breaks-immersion — claim does not hold]
Re-ran the precise off-axis repro (chase party at `(700,800)`, weak hero at `(1050,800)`, both level with each other, offset from the bridge's transverse position): party correctly identifies the hero as the goal, closes distance, and then **freezes at `(985,760)` and stays there for the full 20 simulated seconds I ran it** — `p.stuckT` climbs past the claimed 1.6s trigger and keeps climbing (observed up to 17.05 with zero movement), so the "hard reroute" branch is firing every frame, but it does not help.
**Root cause (verified live):** at the party's exact resting x (`985`), scanning `blockedAt(985, y)` from y=760 down to y=630 shows a blocked ring from y≈735–755, with clear ground on both sides (760 and ≤730) — the same disjoint-radius dead pocket documented in the original mvp-verify addendum, unchanged. The "reroute to nearest bridge" fix picks `(985,640)` as the target (correctly the closest bridge), but the straight line from `(985,760)` to `(985,640)` runs directly through that same dead pocket — because the chase/flee goal keeps the party's x pinned dead-on-axis with the bridge, and the game has no lateral-search/wall-following behavior to discover the clear detour that verifiably exists just 15-50px to either side (scanned `blockedAt(x, 745)` for `x` in `[940,1040]`: blocked only for `x` in `[985,1025]`, wide open at `x≤980` or `x≥1030`). The stuck-timer reroute changes *where* the party aims, not *how* it navigates around a local obstacle, so it cannot solve a case where the straight line to the new target is itself blocked.
**This is the same bug as before, not a narrower version of it** — identical trigger, identical freeze point, identical duration tested (previously 300s, now 20s, both with zero recovery). The claim that "your off-axis river freeze should be impossible now" does not hold under the exact repro that was handed back to me. I'm keeping finding #8 as Breaks-immersion and **not** crediting it as closed.
**Fix (unchanged from prior recommendation):** the stuck-timer reroute needs an actual local escape — e.g., if blocked, try a handful of angular offsets from the direct line (a cheap steering fan) instead of only ever aiming at the single nearest bridge point in a straight line, or give the bridge-exemption radius and the river-block radius a continuous falloff so no dead annulus can exist at any approach angle in the first place (the original, still-correct diagnosis).
**Effort:** M — unchanged from the original estimate; this patch round did not address the actual mechanism.

## Second re-verification addendum ("root-cause river fix" + spawn safety + fled-party memory)

Method: cache-busted reload (`fetch(cache:'reload')` on `/`/`/index.html`, `navigate force:true`), then re-ran the exact repro two ways plus the two new claims, all live.

### 1. River freeze "dead at the root" — NOT VERIFIED. Identical freeze reproduces, twice. [Breaks-immersion — third consecutive failed fix claim]
Read the new `moveBlocked` (`world.js:113-122`): the change adds one guard — `if (this.blockedAt(e.x, e.y)) { e.x = nx; e.y = ny; return; }` — i.e. an entity whose *current* position is already inside a blocked region moves freely until it exits. Confirmed this code is actually loaded live (`w.moveBlocked.toString()` matches).

This guard only fires when the entity is standing **inside** a blocked zone. It does nothing for the actual failure mode I reported, which is an entity resting on **clear ground immediately adjacent to** a blocked pocket where all three of `moveBlocked`'s own fallback slides (straight line, x-only, y-only) land on blocked squares. Re-verified this is exactly what's still happening:

- **Natural repro, run twice, 40 simulated seconds each:** party `(700,800)` chasing hero `(1050,800)` — closes to `(985,760)` in ~3s exactly as before, then sits frozen at `(985,760)` for the full 40s, zero displacement.
- **Direct teleport repro (the coordinator's own suggested variant):** placed the party at `(985,760)` already, already in `mood:'chase'` with velocity already aimed at the hero. Ran 40 simulated seconds. **Zero movement, no battle triggered.** This directly contradicts "verified... engages battle in <40s."
- **Confirmed the resting point itself is not blocked:** `blockedAt(985,760)` returns `false` — so the new "already embedded" guard never activates; the party was never inside the blocked zone, it's parked one step outside it, which is a different bug than the one this patch targets.
- **The dead pocket is unchanged:** re-scanned `blockedAt(985, y)` for y from 760 down to 630 — still blocked for y in [735,755], clear both above and below. Re-scanned `blockedAt(x, 745)` for x in [940,1040] — still blocked only for x in [985,1025], clear at x≤980 or x≥1030. The lateral detour that's always existed is still there; the party still can't find it because nothing in the movement code does anything but aim a straight line at the single nearest bridge point.

This is the **third** time a fix for this exact repro has been reported as landed (bridge-routing pass in mvp-verify, "catch-all unstick via stuckT>1.6" in my first re-score, now this "root cause" pass) and the **third** time the identical repro reproduces the identical permanent freeze at the identical coordinate. I'm not softening this: a claim that specified the exact repro and an exact outcome ("<40s") and turned out to be flatly wrong on a live re-run is a bigger problem than the bug itself — it means "fixed and verified" claims from this pipeline need independent re-testing before they can be trusted, which is precisely what this critique pass is for. Finding #8 stays open, Breaks-immersion, unchanged severity.
**Fix:** still unchanged from both prior write-ups — either give the entity a local lateral-search/steering-fan fallback when the direct line to its chosen goal is blocked (don't only ever aim straight at the single nearest bridge point), or eliminate the dead annulus itself with a continuous falloff between the river-block radius and the bridge-exemption radius so no such pocket can exist at any angle.

### 2. Parties can no longer spawn embedded — VERIFIED

**NOT fixed — my original round-1 repro (the very first freeze ever reported in this critique thread) still never resolves, via a newly-discovered root cause.**
Re-ran the exact original setup — party `(700,800)`, hero `(1050,800)` — for **180 simulated seconds (3 real minutes)**. Result: **never engages battle.** The party is not dead-stopped this time; it's locked in a bounded oscillation, wandering a box roughly `x:[700,1028], y:[786,923]` in `mood:'chase'` the entire time, never approaching the hero closer than ~90-100px.

**Root-caused it myself:** `pathGoal(x,y,goal)` returned `null` at this exact position — meaning Dijkstra found *no route to the goal at all* through any of the 15 nav nodes, so the party just keeps driving its velocity straight at the (river-blocked) hero position every frame, sliding along the bank in a chaotic wobble. Dug into why: `lineClear(x1,y1,x2,y2)` — the visibility primitive the whole nav graph depends on — **is not symmetric**. Directly verified: `lineClear(1050,800 → 1090.36,608.39)` returns `true`, but the reverse, `lineClear(1090.36,608.39 → 1050,800)` (identical physical segment, endpoints swapped), returns `false`. Reproduced the same asymmetry on a second, independent point pair nearby, and confirmed it does *not* happen on pairs farther from a blocked-band edge (two other sampled pairs were consistently symmetric) — so it's a real, situational bug, not a universal break, but a directly demonstrable one. Mechanism (from reading `world.js:170-176`): the sampling loop runs `i=1..n`, which checks the destination endpoint but never samples the immediate vicinity of the *origin* point — so a blocked cell adjacent to whichever point happens to be the *start* of the call silently escapes the check, while the same cell is caught when that point is the *end* of the call instead. Since `pathGoal`'s Dijkstra step computes reachability to the goal as `lineClear(navNode → goal)` (nav-node-as-origin), this asymmetry can make a hero position that is genuinely reachable from a node (proven by the reverse call) register as unreachable *from every node in the graph simultaneously*, collapsing the entire route search to "no path" for that goal — which is exactly what happened here.

This is a structurally different bug from the three previous rounds' disjoint-radius dead pockets — it's a directional flaw in the visibility primitive itself, not a geometry-tuning gap — and it reproduces on the single most basic, most-tested repro in this entire five-round thread. The coordinator's 28-case lattice sweep (both rivers × both directions × 7 latitudes) is real, rigorous coverage, but it tests fixed sample points rather than re-running the specific failing cases already on file, and this particular bug depends on the exact relationship between a *continuous* goal position (the hero, who moves freely) and the graph, not on any of the sampled lattice points — so a sweep built to characterize the map's geometry was never going to catch a bug that lives in the order of arguments to a helper function.
**Fix:** make `lineClear` symmetric — either sample `i=0..n` inclusive of both endpoints (cheapest), or explicitly test both directions and AND them together before trusting a visibility edge. This is a small, surgical fix; the graph/Dijkstra structure around it doesn't need to change.
**Effort:** S — one off-by-one-style loop-bound fix, but it should come with a targeted regression case (goal-directional symmetry check for `lineClear`, not just more lattice points) so this class of bug can't hide behind a sweep again.
