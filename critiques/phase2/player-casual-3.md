# Casual Playtester Session 3 — Bannerfall

**Persona:** Fresh casual player. Likes Stardew, match-3, Kingdom Two Crowns on easy. Never played Thronefall or Bannerlord. Learns by poking around, not manuals. Gets frustrated by unclear UI and deaths that feel unfair. Quits games that waste time.

## Session diary

**0:00 — Title screen.** "BANNERFALL — Raise a warband. Raze the camps. Take Wolfsjaw Hold." Cute mountain silhouettes. Control hints are all dumped on screen at once (WASD, mouse, space, order keys 1/2/3, recruit tips) before I've even pressed a button. A lot to read before playing, but at least it's all in one place instead of hidden in a menu. Pressed Enter to start.

**~0:15 — Dropped into the world.** Top bar shows gold (80), troop count (4/12), and health (120/120). Objective banner: "Raze the bandit camps (0/3) to unlock Wolfsjaw." Clear goal, good. No minimap or compass though — just a village (Ashford) nearby and a bandit camp marker far off. Wandered toward Ashford.

**~0:45 — First village (Ashford).** Standing near it popped a panel: "Village of Ashford — Q Spearman 15g · E Archer 25g · F Rest & heal 10g." Nice, discoverable, no separate shop screen needed. Recruited troops here a few times over the session.

**~1:30 — First random fight.** Wandered near a roaming enemy icon and got pulled into a battle screen (background goes red/pink, "Warband N · Slain X/Y" counter, order buttons FOLLOW/CHARGE/HOLD appear). Wasn't sure I'd "entered combat" until the screen changed color — no fanfare, just suddenly a different mode. Won this one, "Victory, no losses!" popup, gained gold. Felt good, low stakes.

**~2:30 to ~9:00 — The bandit camp wall.** This is where the session turned. Found the actual objective camp (has an "E: Raid the camp (counts toward the 3)" prompt). Recruited up to 10/12 troops, walked in, hit E, and got dropped into a real fight against 6-9 enemies.

I tried this fight **four separate times**, changing my approach each time:
1. Full warband (10 troops), CHARGE order — wiped down to 0 troops mid-fight, had to flee.
2. Rebuilt to 3 troops, HOLD order, personally clicked at an enemy — same result: troops gone, forced retreat.
3. Rebuilt again, FOLLOW order this time, moved in and attacked directly — same result again.
4. One more attempt, same again.

Every single time I came out of the fight with **almost exactly the same numbers**: about half my max HP gone, my troops reduced from however many I brought down to 2, and a small trickle of gold (10-15g), with the objective still stuck at (0/3). Changing tactics (charge vs. hold vs. follow, fighting personally vs. not) didn't seem to change the outcome at all. There's a "ride west: RETREAT" prompt that appears once you're critically low, which was at least a clear, welcome safety valve — but I never once got past this camp, not even close.

Between fights I'd trudge back to the village, spend my scraped-together gold on 10g heals and 15g spearmen, then walk back and lose them again. It felt like running on a treadmill: recruit, walk, lose troops, retreat, recruit, walk, lose troops, retreat. Four attempts with no visible progress and no feedback on *why* I kept losing (was my formation wrong? Was I under-leveled? Do I need a specific troop type?) is exactly the kind of "unfair, wastes my time" loop that makes me stop playing.

## Confusion list
- No minimap/compass — landmarks (Ashford, Brindle, the bandit camp) are close together and I kept getting turned around walking between the same three points.
- No indicator of *why* my troops die so fast in the camp fights, or what troop composition/numbers would actually be "enough." The enemy count badges (4, 6, 9) give no sense of how they compare to my own strength number.
- CHARGE vs. HOLD vs. FOLLOW orders didn't seem to visibly change anything about how the fight played out for me — no clear feedback loop for a first-timer to learn from.
- Wasn't sure at first whether clicking near an enemy was actually landing hits — no visible hit-flash/damage number I noticed.

## Delight list
- The recruit panel at villages is genuinely good UX: walk up, see prices, tap a letter key, done. No sub-menu.
- Numbers update instantly and visibly (gold, troop count, HP) right after recruiting/healing — good, legible feedback for that part of the loop.
- The "Victory, no losses!" popup for the easy first encounter felt like a nice small reward.
- The retreat prompt ("ride west: RETREAT") appearing when health gets critical is a fair, clear safety net once you notice it.

## Rage moments
- ~2:30–9:00: repeatedly getting stomped by the exact same bandit camp with no visible way to improve my odds, four times in a row, with almost identical bad outcomes each time regardless of tactics tried. This is the moment I'd genuinely stop playing.

## Quit-or-hooked verdict

**Quit.** The flip moment was the third failed attempt at the bandit camp — by then it was clear this wasn't bad luck, it was a wall I didn't have the tools or information to get past. A casual player in my position doesn't have a spreadsheet-brain readiness to min-max troop comps; I want the game to telegraph "you're not ready, go earn more gold/troops elsewhere first" or to make the first camp forgiving enough to learn the ropes. Instead it repeatedly took most of my warband and half my health for a few gold, with the core objective counter never moving off 0/3.

## Recommend to a friend?

No, not in its current state. I'd say "it looks charming and the shopping-at-villages part is nice, but the first real fight you're supposed to do just isn't winnable for me and I couldn't tell why — skip it for now."

## Fun score: 3/10

The opening ten seconds (title, clear one-line goal, recruiting at a village) are genuinely appealing and low-friction — exactly my kind of casual pick-up-and-play. But the moment the game asks you to do the one thing it tells you to do (raze a bandit camp), it turns into a brick wall with no visible feedback on how to improve, and repeating the same losing fight four times in a row killed my motivation completely. A relaxing-game player like me needs early wins to build confidence; instead I got repeated, unexplained defeats that felt arbitrary rather than skill-based, which is the fastest way to get me to close the tab.
