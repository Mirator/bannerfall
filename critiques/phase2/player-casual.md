# Bannerfall — Casual Player Diary (Playtester #1)

Persona reminder: I play Stardew Valley, match-3, Kingdom Two Crowns on easy. I have never played Thronefall or Bannerlord. I don't read manuals. I get frustrated fast by unclear UI and deaths that feel unfair, and I quit games that waste my time.

## Diary

**0:00 — Title screen.** Big friendly title, "Raise a warband. Raze the camps. Take Wolfsjaw Hold." Cute mountain silhouettes. There's a wall of text at the bottom (WASD ride, mouse aim, LMB swing, SPACE dash, troop orders 1/2/3, "recruit in villages, pick fights you can win"). I would normally skip all of this, but I skimmed it because it's short. Pressed Enter.

**0:05 — Dropped into the map.** Top-left HUD: gold, a sword+number (troops), a heart+number (health). Top-right: "Take Wolfsjaw Hold · camps razed 0/3." I saw a village called Ashford nearby and two red enemy blobs with numbers on them across a river. No tutorial arrow, no "go here first" — I just picked a direction.

**0:30 — Found Ashford.** A little popup: "Q Spearman 15g · E Archer 25g · F Rest & heal 10g." Clear and simple, like a shop menu in a match-3 game. I bought a spearman and an archer. Gold dropped from 80 to 40, troop count went from 4/12 to 6/12. This felt GOOD — instant, readable feedback ("Spearman joined your warband" banner). This is the best-designed moment of the whole session.

**1:30 — First fight.** Walked into a "Bandit camp" prompt, pressed E to attack, got dropped into a zoomed-in combat view with a health/stamina bar and the same 1/FOLLOW 2/CHARGE 3/HOLD buttons at the bottom. I hit "2 CHARGE." My troops piled onto a bridge chokepoint against the bandits and mowed them down — "Slain 4/5" then victory, back to the map with +35 gold. Satisfying, looked like a tug-of-war at a bridge, easy to read.

**3:00 — Second fight, everything fell apart.** I found a bigger red/pink territory with 6 enemies scattered in separate little clumps instead of one group. I hit CHARGE again since it worked last time. My troops sprinted off ahead of me into two or three separate enemy pods at once and got shredded — my warband count dropped from 5 to 2 to 0 in about 15 seconds of game time, while I was still walking over from the flag. I never got a clear look at what killed them. My own health bar also dropped to nearly empty from a fight I didn't feel like I was really "in." I ended up back on the world map having lost almost my whole warband and half my health, for no gold reward.

**5:00 — Broke.** Gold was down to 9. Rest & Heal costs 10g. I literally could not afford to heal. "Not enough gold" message. This is the moment a Stardew-brain player goes "oh no, I've soft-locked myself" — no obvious way to earn gold except fighting, and fighting is what just wrecked me.

**6:00 — Tried again anyway.** Went looking for a smaller enemy group ("4" or "5" shown on their icon) hoping for an easier win to recover gold. Found a camp, this time tried following my troops in more carefully and fighting some enemies myself directly with mouse-aim + click. Got a couple of kills (2-3/5), but again ended the encounter with 0 troops left and my health bar reduced to a sliver of red. Retreated to the map automatically — no permadeath screen, just dumped back near Ashford with 2/12 troops, 6 gold, 60/120 health.

**~8:00 — Stopping point.** At this point I'm essentially stuck: 2 troops, 6 gold, half health, and the last two fights taught me nothing concrete about how to avoid losing my whole warband again. This is where I, playing as this persona, would put the controller down. "Camps razed" was still 0/3 despite me clearing at least three separate "Bandit camp" locations, so I also don't know if any of that fighting even counted toward the actual goal.

## Confusion list (in order encountered)

1. **What is the flag-and-numbers icon?** I initially thought the little person with a flag and a number on the map was a separate object (a "recruit available" marker) rather than realizing it was ME, the player character, with my troop count next to it. Took one movement test to confirm.
2. **"Camps razed 0/3" never moved.** I defeated at least three distinct "Bandit camp" markers over the session and the counter stayed at 0/3 the entire time. Either those don't count toward the objective, or the counter is broken, or there's a different kind of "camp" I never found. I have no idea which, and the game never told me.
3. **Why did CHARGE work great once and catastrophically badly the second time?** No difference was telegraphed between the bridge fight (one enemy cluster, chokepoint, easy win) and the open-field fight (multiple scattered enemy pods, my troops died fleeing to fight them separately). I had no way to know in advance that the second fight's enemy layout would punish the same order that worked before.
4. **How much danger am I actually in?** Enemy icons show numbers ("D 3", a wrench icon with "2", etc.) that I assume are troop counts or damage values, but I never figured out what they meant well enough to judge "can I win this" before committing, which the loading-screen tip literally told me to do ("pick fights you can win") without giving me the information to do it.
5. **Why did my warband go from 5 to 0 in seconds while I was still walking toward the fight?** It felt like my troops were dying in a fight I wasn't watching or participating in. No death recap, no "your archer was flanked" moment — just numbers ticking down.
6. **Gold economy dead end.** Getting to 9 gold with a 10-gold heal cost and no clear cheap way to earn gold outside of fights I kept losing felt like a trap with no signposted way out.

## Delight list

- Recruiting at Ashford: instant, clear, satisfying feedback ("Spearman joined your warband," numbers updating live). Best UI moment in the game.
- The first bridge fight: a clean, readable tug-of-war at a chokepoint that I could actually follow with my eyes and understand why I won.
- No punishing death screen — losing a fight just dumps you back on the map near your last village instead of a game over. That softened the blow a lot; I didn't feel like I "lost the game," just a skirmish.
- Discovering a castle (Highmere) and a second village (Brindle) while wandering gave a small "ooh, more map" moment.

## Rage list

- Losing an entire warband (5 troops) in one open-field fight with zero warning it would go differently than the fight before it.
- Being unable to afford to heal after two bad fights in a row, with no clear way to earn money except more fighting.
- The "camps razed" counter not updating after clearing camps — feels like my progress toward the actual goal (Take Wolfsjaw Hold) evaporated.
- CHARGE sending my troops ahead of me to fight without me, so I couldn't help or intervene before they died.

## Would I quit for good?

Yes — around the 8-minute mark, once I was down to 2 troops, 6 gold, and half health with no clear next move and no visible progress on the actual objective. I kept playing a little past that only because I was asked to test a full session; a real session of mine would have ended there. The early game (recruiting, first fight) hooked me; the second and third fights un-hooked me by feeling random and unexplained.

## Would I recommend it to a friend like me?

Not in its current state. I'd tell a friend "the recruiting and the first fight are cute, but it gets mean fast and you can't tell why, and then you can't afford to fix it." I'd recommend it more to someone who likes tactics/strategy games and wants to study enemy formations before engaging — not to someone who wants to relax.

## Fun score: 4/10

The opening loop (walk, recruit, one clean fight) is genuinely charming and about a 7 or 8 — simple, readable, rewarding. But the game has no easing-in: the second real fight I found threw scattered multi-group enemies at me with the same tools and the same lack of information as the trivial first fight, and it cost me almost my entire warband and half my health for no reward. Combined with a gold economy that let me bottom out into "can't afford to heal, can't win a fight, don't know what actually counts toward the goal," the middle of the session felt punishing rather than challenging. A casual player will bounce off the second time their warband evaporates without understanding why.
